LITEL-01 · IN BRING-UP

LiTel, a vehicle telemetry link

The old telemetry link was heavy, ran 1 to 30 seconds behind the car, dropped most channels, and fell over if more than about two people opened the data at once — so the team stopped fitting it, and the last two cars ran without telemetry. LiTel is the replacement: a four-layer mixed-signal board around an STM32H533 that ingests classic CAN at 1 Mbit/s and relays frames over a 900 MHz RFD900ux link to a ground station in the pit.

Implementation notes
  • Wide-Vin automotive front end — TVS, fuse, reverse-polarity P-FET, TPS54360-Q1 buck — running straight off the vehicle low-voltage bus.
  • Interrupt-driven CAN receive with a full-FIFO drain into a lock-free ring buffer, then CRC-checked COBS framing over the radio.
  • Budgeted for sub-200 ms end to end. The board is a relay, not a logger — the M150 keeps the authoritative log.

LISS-01 · FINISHED MAY 2026

Lissajous signal generator

A mixed-signal board that generates phase- and frequency-controlled stereo sine waves and streams them over I2S to a PCM5102A, so an oscilloscope in XY mode draws Lissajous figures.

Implementation notes
  • FreeRTOS firmware with a dual-mode state machine: internal waveform generation, or USB audio passthrough from a host.
  • Ring-buffered audio out, plus ADC conditioning on the controls with hysteresis and an EMA filter so the figures hold still.
  • Fabricated at JLCPCB and hand-assembled. It came up on the first power-on; I verified the rails, I2S timing, and DAC output on the scope before trusting a single sample.

CANX-01 · PROOF OF CONCEPT · JAN 2026

Miniature CAN expansion board

My first board for the team, drawn as low voltage engineer: a proof of concept for turning eight analog and two digital sensor inputs into scheduled CAN frames. It never went through bring-up and never ran on the car.

Implementation notes
  • STM32C092 with a TCAN3404-Q1 transceiver and a TPS542025 buck, sized to tuck into the space the harness already had.
  • HAL firmware to sample, scale, and schedule each channel onto the bus at its own rate rather than flooding it. Written, not yet exercised against hardware.
  • Analog conditioning — RC filters, dividers, op-amp buffers — simulated in LTspice against the real sensor ranges before layout.
  • Where it stopped: the schematic, layout, and firmware are done, but the team's needs moved to LiTel before I took this one through bring-up. The groundwork carried into the 2027 EV platform expansion board.

How I got here

I joined Panther Racing as a low voltage engineer, drew a CAN board and a dashboard harness, and found I liked the projects where the firmware has to answer to real physics — signal integrity, power budgets, timing margin, sensor noise.

A year later I took over the driverless subteam, which had been dormant since 2024, and have been building its software and its hardware case ever since. Between those I keep a Linux server at home running my own services, and I teach sixty freshmen C and MATLAB as a TA for ENGR 0711.

Away from a bench I cook and bake for the people around me, and I read in the rain on purpose.
